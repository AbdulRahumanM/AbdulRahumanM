# Project Aster — Physician Shadowing Recruitment Brief

Reference brief used to build the warm-intro message template and the shadowing
recruitment landing page (`index.html`).

## About the project

Project Aster is an early-stage AI copilot concept for independent primary
care practices (3–20 physicians). It prioritizes incoming patient messages
and drafts protocol-based responses for physician review. The business model
is tied to US-specific dynamics: CMS APCM/CCM billing codes, MIPS reporting,
and distribution through athenahealth/eClinicalWorks (the dominant EHRs for
this practice segment).

Before building anything, the founder (Abdul, based in Sandy Springs, GA — 7
years in health tech, most recently as Principal PM at Twin Health) is
running a pre-product research phase: shadowing 10 independent primary care
clinics to observe message-handling workflow firsthand. Target mix: 6
Internal Medicine, 2 Family Medicine, 2 DPC/concierge practices.

**Longer-term product thesis** (framing, not necessarily for the page
itself): physician edits/accepts/rejects of drafted messages function as a
reward signal — similar to how coding tools like Cursor use accept/revert
actions in production to continuously improve their models. Aggregated
across many practices over time, this becomes a data moat competitors can't
easily replicate. Shadowing now is the first step toward understanding
whether that signal is clean enough to build on.

## Recruiting approach

- **In-person (6–7 slots):** Atlanta metro practices, sourced via Georgia
  medical society chapters, NPI registry filtered to local counties, and
  direct outreach to practice managers.
- **Remote (3–4 slots):** screen-share sessions where a physician narrates
  while triaging their actual inbox — used to sanity-check that
  Atlanta-based observations aren't a regional quirk. Sourced through
  Abdul's existing health-tech network, scattered across other US states.
- Practice managers are the gatekeepers and should often be contacted before
  the physician.
- A landing page / one-pager isn't the acquisition channel — warm intros and
  direct outreach are. The page is a trust artifact sent after a warm intro
  lands, to address objections before a call.

## What the warm-intro message needs to do

A short message Abdul's health-tech connects can forward to physician
contacts they know. Should include:

- Brief credibility: who Abdul is, health-tech background (Twin Health, 7
  years in the space)
- One-sentence description of Aster (AI copilot helping independent
  practices handle patient message volume)
- The specific, low-friction ask: a 30–60 min shadowing session (in-person
  if local to Atlanta, or a remote screen-share) — observing, not
  disrupting their workflow
- Explicit no-obligation framing — this is research, not a sales pitch
- A link to the landing page/one-pager for more detail

## What the landing page (or extension) needs to cover

- Who Abdul is and why he's doing this (credibility + intent)
- What shadowing actually involves — format (in-person vs. remote), time
  commitment, what he's looking to observe
- How privacy/PHI exposure is handled — this objection surfaces before
  physicians agree, so it needs a clear, upfront answer even in this
  pre-product phase
- What's in it for them — options considered: early access/input into the
  eventual product, or a modest honorarium for their time
- Simple next step (calendar link or reply-to-schedule)

## Tone

Direct, low-pressure, credible-peer-to-physician — not a startup pitch.
Physicians are time-constrained and skeptical of vendor asks; the message
should read as "I want to learn from watching you work," not "I want to
sell you something."
