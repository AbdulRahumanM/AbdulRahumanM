Project Aster · Pre-product research

I'd like to watch how your practice handles patient messages — no pitch, no obligation.

I'm Abdul, a health-tech product person spending 7 years building clinical software, most recently as Principal PM at Twin Health. Before building anything new, I want to understand how independent primary care practices actually triage the flood of patient messages — by watching, not by guessing.

Why I'm doing this

I'm exploring an early concept — Project Aster — for an AI copilot that helps independent practices (3–20 physicians) keep up with incoming patient message volume: prioritizing what's urgent and drafting protocol-based responses for a physician to review, edit, or reject.

That's a hypothesis, not a finished product. Before I build anything, I want to shadow about 10 practices to see the real workflow: what's actually time-consuming, what's already working, and where a tool like this would help or just get in the way. This page exists so you have the details in writing before we talk.

What shadowing actually involves

In-person

If you're in the Atlanta metro area, I'll come to your office and observe in person — one session, no repeat visits needed.

Remote

If you're elsewhere, this works just as well as a screen-share call, where you narrate as you triage your actual inbox.

  • Time: 30–60 minutes, scheduled around your day.
  • What I'm looking for: how messages get triaged and routed, what takes the most time, what's already handled well, and where the friction actually is — in your own workflow, not a hypothetical one.
  • What I'm not doing: disrupting your workflow, recording patient data, or pitching a product for you to buy.

How I handle privacy and PHI

This is a genuine, upfront concern and I want to answer it before you have to ask:

  • I observe workflow and process — how messages move, get prioritized, and get answered — not the content of specific patient records.
  • No PHI is recorded, photographed, or written down. If a message happens to be visible on screen, it's incidental to observing the process, never the point of the exercise.
  • Nothing from the session is shared outside this research — no names, no practice-identifying details, in any notes or future product materials, without your explicit sign-off.
  • There's no product yet, no data pipeline, no integration — this is me in the room (or on a call) with a notebook, not software touching your system.

What's in it for you

  • Early input: if this becomes a real product, the practices I shadow now shape what gets built first — and get early access ahead of anyone else.
  • A modest honorarium for your time, regardless of whether anything comes of the broader project.
  • No commitment beyond the session — this is a one-time observation, not the start of a sales relationship.
